# AI Field — Three Design Directions

> Concept exploration (Impeccable new-work, pre-lock). Three directions that differ **primarily in spatial composition, interaction model, hierarchy, navigation philosophy, and progression** — not in typeface or theme. No exact fonts or colors chosen yet. Grounded in `EXPERIENCE-THESIS.md` and screened against `ANTI-PATTERNS.md`. Nothing is built. The recommended direction still owes the Impeccable concept-seed lock + comp round (where fonts/colors are decided) once chosen.
>
> Audience reminder: **non-technical professionals** learning to direct AI. Usability and low intimidation are first-class constraints.

The three at a glance — they diverge on navigation philosophy and progression:

| | **A · The Bench** | **B · The Session Line** | **C · The Standing Assignment** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Navigation | **Spatial** — places you move between | **Temporal** — a time-spine you travel | **Singular** — one thread, near-zero nav |
| Interaction | Direct manipulation (place materials) | Build forward, then scrub back | Accept → work → debrief → next |
| Hierarchy | Proximity & scale (front vs. periphery) | The active moment on a continuous line | Radical single-focus; history recedes |
| Progression | The reach/state of your practice | A longitudinal, evidence-anchored thread | A lineage of completed work + verdicts |

---

## Direction A — The Bench

**Concept.** A craftsperson's work surface, not an app: you always stand at a place where the current job is laid out under your hands.
*Thesis: AI Field is somewhere you work, not something you browse — every surface is a bench with the job in front of you and your materials within reach.*

**Mental model.** The *behavior* of working at a physical surface — proximity means active, aside means reference, and you lay out materials before you use them. (Not a skeuomorphic woodshop; the model is spatial behavior, not wood texture.)

**The Field.**
- *Available missions:* a small "yard" of benches — each mission is a work surface you can step up to, shown at rest, not a card in a grid. You approach one; you don't scan a catalog.
- *Recommended without a giant card:* the recommended bench is simply the one already **pulled forward and lit for work** — dominance by depth and readiness (it's the surface facing you), not by being a bigger rectangle with a badge.
- *Progression without a stats widget:* your capability shows as the **reach of your practice** — a quiet spatial spread around your bench of what you now handle well versus what's still out of reach, read as territory worked, not five meters. The five competencies are labels on that spread, surfaced on attention, never a chart.

**Briefing → doing.** Stepping up to a bench is the transition: the yard falls away, the surface comes forward and settles, the brief is pinned at the edge of the surface where a work-order would sit — you're already *at* the bench before you start, so beginning is picking up the first material, not clicking "Start Mission."

**Workbench (spatial relationship).** One continuous surface, no floating panels. **Mission info** is pinned at the top edge (the work-order, always in view). **Source resources** rest to one side, physically *outside* the AI's reach until you place them. The **AI** occupies the working middle-ground as a capable instrument you address. **Your instructions** are the tool in your hand — a single deliberate input, not a chat composer. The **evolving deliverable** is the piece taking shape directly under you, on the near part of the surface, assembled by drawing fragments of AI output down onto it. It reads as an instrument because everything is one workspace with real spatial roles — nothing is "a chat with a panel next to it."

**Debrief (narrative).** The examiner walks your bench with you: the surface replays as the observer moves across it, pausing where you made a decision, so feedback arrives *in the order you worked* and attached to the spot it happened — one continuous walkthrough, not three parallel cards.

**Visual composition.** High spatial density with generous quiet at the edges; whitespace is *space around the work*, not padding inside cards. Typography functional and confident — one workhorse face carrying display and body through weight/size, no decorative pairing. Borders mostly absent; zones are defined by inset, elevation, and subtle surface shifts rather than outlines. Geometry orthogonal and grounded. Almost no containers — the surface *is* the container. Iconography minimal, drawn in the system's own line grammar (no ✨). Imagery: restrained material/tactile cues at most; no decorative gradients.

**Motion.**
1. *Attach* — a resource slides from the side into the AI's field and stays there (cause/effect: context is now something you can see the AI has).
2. *Enter a bench* — the surface pushes forward from the yard and settles into working distance (spatial movement).
3. *Assemble* — a pulled fragment of AI output travels down and seats into the deliverable (state change you can watch).

**Signature interaction.** **Laying materials into the AI's field** — the deliberate act of moving a resource across the bench into the AI's reach (and the fact that un-placed materials simply aren't available to it). Context — the hardest, most-measured skill — becomes a physical gesture you feel responsible for.

**Risk.** Slides toward a gimmicky canvas or skeuomorphic set-dressing; drag-and-place is fiddly and intimidating for non-technical users, hard to make robust in a vibe-coded MVP, and spatial metaphors tend to break on mobile. If the "yard" grows literal (paths, plots), it becomes the map we banned.

## Direction B — The Session Line

**Concept.** Your work is a session that runs along a line; afterwards an expert travels that same line with you and tells the truth at each point.
*Thesis: AI Field organizes everything around the session as a thing in time — you build it going forward, and you're shown the truth of it coming back.*

**Mental model.** A coach or editor reviewing a recording *with* you — the practice of reviewing your own work along time. It maps exactly to how the examiner really reasons: evidence pinned to moments (turn ids), never floating claims.

**The Field.**
- *Available missions:* your sessions read as segments on a running line — completed reps behind you, and the next one queued ahead as an open segment ready to run. Missions are "sessions to run," not tiles to browse.
- *Recommended without a giant card:* the next rep is the **one open, forward segment the line points at** — dominance by position and momentum (it's where the line is heading), not by size.
- *Progression without a stats widget:* capability is legible in the **line itself** — the five competencies read as threads that *thicken where you demonstrated them*, visibly building across your annotated sessions. It's a record of evidence over time, not a radar chart or percentage bars.

**Briefing → doing.** The briefing is the **head of the segment** — the moment the line goes from reviewable history to a live, empty stretch waiting to be filled. Entering is committing to start recording; the psychological shift is "the tape is now rolling and this stretch is mine to fill."

**Workbench (spatial relationship).** The session line runs as a spine through the workspace. **Mission info** anchors the head of the line, always visible. **Source resources** dock beside the line as things you can commit into it. The **AI** and **your instructions** are the live head of the line where the current exchange is being laid down — your instruction is a deliberate directive that advances the line, not a chat turn in a bubble stream. The **evolving deliverable** builds alongside the spine as the durable output you're extracting from the run. Instrument, not chatbot, because the organizing object is the *session being recorded*, and chat is just what the live head happens to look like.

**Debrief (narrative).** A **playback**: a marker travels your session line and the examiner's observations surface at the exact points they refer to, in sequence — the story of how you worked, told along the same line you worked on, with the moved competency threads thickening at the moment of evidence. One traveling narrative, not three static cards.

**Visual composition.** Medium density with strong horizontal rhythm; whitespace used to separate moments along the line. Typography clean and editorial-neutral (no serif-editorial cosplay), with small, exact labels for moments. Borders as fine structural rules — the line and its ticks — rather than boxed containers. Surfaces flat and calm so the line reads as the figure. Geometry linear and precise. Containers minimal: moments expand *from* the line, not cards laid *on* it. Iconography tiny and functional (markers, ticks). Imagery: essentially none; the line and its annotations are the graphic.

**Motion.**
1. *Submit* — the live session collapses into a compact completed segment on the line (progression: your work becomes history).
2. *Playback* — the review marker travels the spine and notes rise at their points (cause/effect + spatial: feedback tied to place).
3. *Capability moves* — a competency thread thickens precisely at the moment of evidence during playback (state change earned at a real point).

**Signature interaction.** **One line, two directions** — you build the session forward, and the examiner annotates the *same* line coming back; evidence expands from points you can open. The object you made is the object you're judged along.

**Risk.** Tips into an analytics timeline, a video-editor UI, or a Git-style activity feed — i.e. data viz, which violates "no stats widget." Scrubbing can feel like homework. If the line becomes a progress meter, it becomes the gamified widget we banned.

## Direction C — The Standing Assignment  ← recommended

**Concept.** A demanding mentor gives you one real assignment at a time, watches how you handle it, and debriefs you straight — a continuing relationship, not a course catalog.
*Thesis: AI Field is always exactly one brief in front of you and the honest record of how you handled the last ones — the product is your practice, not a place that stores it.*

**Mental model.** Apprenticeship under an exacting mentor: one real job at a time, watched, and returned with judgment. A relationship, not a library. (Subtle — no mentor avatar, no persona chat.)

**The Field.**
- *Available missions:* by default there is **no grid to browse** — the app *is* your current standing assignment, with your completed reps reading beneath it as a lineage. "Other work available" exists as a quiet, deliberately secondary pullout for when you want to choose off-plan, so choice is preserved without a dashboard.
- *Recommended without a giant card:* the recommendation doesn't sit *in* a card — it **is the surface**. The next rep is the only prominent thing in front of you; dominance by singular focus and the absence of competitors, not by a highlighted tile.
- *Progression without a stats widget:* capability is discovered by **reading back your own lineage** — each completed assignment carries the verdict on how you worked and which capabilities it demonstrated, and the next assignment states your current gap as its *reason for existing*. Bands live as quiet annotations within the lineage, never a headline chart.

**Briefing → doing.** The briefing is the mentor handing you the job; accepting it **clears the room** — the lineage and everything else recede, and the focused work mode opens as the only thing present. The transition from browsing to doing is total: there was never much to browse, and now there's nothing but the work.

**Workbench (spatial relationship).** A focused single-purpose mode, not a dashboard. **Mission info** is the standing brief held at the top, unchanging. **Source resources** are held to one side as things you consciously commit to the AI (attach-by-choice, preserved). The **AI** is the instrument you're directing in the working center. **Your instructions** are a single deliberate directive line — the product's one input, framed as *directing*, not chatting. The **evolving deliverable** is the thing you're accountable for, held opposite the working area and built by pulling in and refining. Instrument, not chatbot+panel, because the whole mode is organized around producing *the deliverable you'll put your name on*, with the AI subordinate to that goal rather than being the main event.

**Debrief (narrative).** The mentor's read unfolds **progressively in place**, in the order of the work: what you did → the moment it turned → what a stronger operator would have done → the one skill you showed → the gap that becomes your next assignment — each beat revealed in sequence and tied to a real moment, resolving into the next brief. A single spoken-through narrative, not three parallel feedback cards. *(Recommended raise: run this narrative along Direction B's session line, so each beat anchors to the exact turn it refers to — the honest-debrief moment made evidentiary.)*

**Visual composition.** Low density, high intent; abundant whitespace used as *focus*, not decoration — the page is quiet because only one thing matters at a time. Typography with real presence for the brief and the debrief (the two voice-bearing surfaces), workhorse clarity for the work — carried by one confident face across weights, not a decorative pairing. Borders sparing; separation by space and weight, not boxes. Surfaces calm and matte, few in number. Geometry upright and composed. Containers almost absent — the single focus removes the need to fence regions. Iconography minimal and functional. Imagery: typographic and compositional rather than illustrative; no decorative AI gradients.

**Motion.**
1. *Handoff* — on submit, the current assignment rises into the lineage as completed while the next assignment settles into focus (progression + spatial: the practice advances one rep).
2. *Clear the room* — accepting a brief recedes the surroundings and opens the focused work mode (state change: stepping *into* the work).
3. *The read* — debrief beats reveal in sequence, each pausing on the moment it names (cause/effect: judgment attached to what you actually did).

**Signature interaction.** **The next rep rises.** Submitting resolves into one quietly ceremonious moment where the debrief names your gap and the next assignment *becomes* the only thing in front of you. The app is always "one brief," and finishing one is inseparable from receiving the next.

**Risk.** Its worst failure is reading as a **chat thread or feed** — the exact ChatGPT-wrapper anti-pattern — if the lineage isn't unmistakably a record of *work with verdicts* rather than messages. Too-linear can frustrate users who want to pick; the secondary "other work" pullout must be genuinely reachable. And the mentor framing tips **patronizing** the instant the voice softens from exacting-peer to encouraging-teacher.

## Comparison & recommendation

Scored 1–5 (5 = strongest) on the axes you named.

| Axis | A · Bench | B · Session Line | C · Standing Assignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distinctiveness | **5** | 4 | 3½ (conceptual, not novel layout) |
| Usability (non-technical) | 2½ | 4 | **5** |
| Fit with product thesis | 4 | 4½ | **5** |
| Feasibility (vibe-coded MVP) | 2 | 3½ | **5** |
| Scale to future missions | 3½ | 4 | **4½** |

**Reasoning.** **A (Bench)** is the boldest and most tactile — "instrument for working" is literal here — but it's the riskiest to execute well: direct manipulation is fiddly, intimidating for non-technical users, expensive to make robust in a fast MVP, and fragile on mobile. **B (Session Line)** is elegant precisely because it *is* the examiner's evidence model made visible, and its debrief-as-narrative is native — but its constant risk is sliding into analytics/data-viz, the thing our thesis forbids. **C (Standing Assignment)** most directly and most cheaply delivers the thesis — trusted practitioner, one clear next rep, capability discovered through evidence — and it attacks the dashboard/LMS anti-pattern hardest by refusing to be a browse surface at all. It's the most usable for the audience and the most feasible to vibe-code.

**Recommendation: Direction C, raised by Direction B.** Build the Standing Assignment as the backbone, and adopt B's session line specifically as the **debrief/evidence mechanism** — so the honest read is anchored to the exact moments it refers to. That fuses C's usability, feasibility, and thesis-fit with B's evidentiary "seen and told the truth" moment, while designing hard against C's one real danger (reading as a chat feed): the lineage must always present as *work-with-verdicts*, never messages.

This is a recommendation, not a decision. **Choose the direction you want and I'll take it into the Impeccable lock + comp round** (that's where the visual world, fonts, and colors get committed).
